# Past work, gates and green or red flags

What a user says about past work is the best evidence for their criteria. A person who left a
job over constant on-call knows something no weight from 1 to 10 captures. This skill turns
those statements into gates and flags, and keeps the user's words next to them.

## Words used here

- **Gate**: a requirement a posting must pass; in the tracker, a requirement of kind `gate`.
- **Green flag**: a sign in a posting that predicts a good fit. It raises a soft requirement's
  rating, or becomes a soft requirement with a weight.
- **Red flag**: a sign that predicts a bad fit. It lowers a rating, or, when the user would
  never accept it, becomes a gate.

## What a user can tell you

Invite these one at a time, and follow the one the user has energy for:

- **A job or project they liked**, and what made it good: the work itself, the team, the pace,
  the manager, what they learned, how decisions were made.
- **A job or project they left or would not repeat**, and the moment they knew.
- **What they are proud of**: work they would like to do again, and skills they want to use.
- **Skills they have but do not want to use** any more.
- **Practical limits**: hours, travel, location, contract type. These are often gates; keep the
  specifics in the user's own criteria set or notes, never in committed files.
- **How they want to grow**: a level, a field, a kind of responsibility.
- **What they hear in interviews that worries or reassures them.**

## Recording it

1. **Write down what they said, in their words.** With a notebook configured, add it to their
   background notebook with `notebook_note_add`, titled by topic ("Past work: what I left",
   "Wants: team"). Without one, keep it in the conversation and tell the user it is not stored.
2. **Propose the mapping, and ask.** Say which requirement the statement touches and how:
   "You said you left because of constant on-call. I'd add a red flag for on-call without a
   rota, and ask whether it is a gate." Change nothing until they agree.
3. **Apply it in a new criteria version** (see the criteria-weighting skill), so earlier scores
   stay readable.

## Mapping statements

| The user says | Likely becomes |
|---|---|
| "I would never again…" | A gate candidate; check it with the deal-breaker question |
| "The best part was…" | A green flag on an existing soft requirement, or a new one |
| "I left because…" | A red flag; a gate if it was the whole reason |
| "I'm fine either way about…" | Weight 0: recorded, not scored |
| "It depends on…" | A soft requirement whose anchors name the condition |

## Checking the result

Score two or three postings the user knows, and read the limiting factor of each aloud. When a
flag fires where the user disagrees, the anchor is too broad; ask which part of the posting
they would point to instead.
